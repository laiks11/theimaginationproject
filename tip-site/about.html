About · Why TIP

We exist so the future is written in every language.

The Imagination Project is a youth-led nonprofit translating AI education for the communities the AI boom left out — and building the skills, research, and rooms where young people shape what technology becomes.

The problem

AI is moving faster than the people it will change most

The knowledge shaping this decade is concentrated in one language, a handful of institutions, and rooms young people are never invited into.

The divide is linguistic.

The overwhelming majority of AI education, safety research, and policy debate exists only in English — locking out most of the world's students before they start.

The skills are eroding.

The habits AI most rewards us for outsourcing — critical thinking, writing, judgment, creative struggle — are exactly the ones young people need to govern it.

The room is closed.

Young people will live longest with today's technology decisions, yet have the least say in them. They are treated as an audience, not a stakeholder.

The solution

Translate. Teach. Investigate.

We attack the divide from three sides: student translators carry AI education into 50+ languages, our programs build the skills AI erodes, and our investigative research holds institutions honest about how they actually use AI. An education hub — half-asynchronous, half-live, built around career paths and group projects — is coming soon to tie it all together.

How change happens

Our theory, summarized

If young people can access AI education in their own language, and practice the human skills AI erodes, then they gain real agency over the technology shaping their lives — and institutions begin treating youth as stakeholders in technology governance, not spectators.

Access creates literacy. Literacy creates voice. Voice, exercised in public — through translation, research, and real projects — changes who gets to write the future.

Translate first. Student translators, earning service hours, carry AI education across the digital divide — to kids, to classrooms, to developing countries.

Teach through making. Programs like Dear Future Leader treat students as authors: group projects, real audiences, skills AI can't replace.

Investigate in public. With data scientists, we're building a precise index of how colleges use and rely on AI — designed to expand across US campuses.

Then convene. The education hub — speakers, podcasts, mentorship, career-path learning — turns a network of programs into a movement.

By 2030: AI education available in the world's major languages; an AI index trusted across US colleges; a hub where a student in any country can find a mentor, a cohort, and a career path — and TIP alumni sitting in the rooms where technology decisions get made.

Values

What we refuse to compromise

V.01Access is non-negotiable

If it isn't translated, it isn't finished. Language should never be the price of admission to the future.

V.02Youth are stakeholders

Not an audience, not a photo op. Young people belong in the rooms where technology is governed.

V.03Imagination is a skill

Creativity, judgment, and critical thought are trainable — and they are exactly what AI cannot replace.

Leadership

The people behind it

Founder

Laika Kalyani Patel

Founder · Yale University

Laika founded The Imagination Project in 2025 — the same month she was crowned Miss Texas Teen USA. Within four months she had written and delivered the Dear Future Leader curriculum to more than five hundred elementary students across Texas, and the accompanying children's book now funds literacy and hygiene programs for over eight hundred women served by Manav Sadhna in Ahmedabad, India.

A former President of Texas DECA, she brings experience across business, cultural exchange, service, and the arts. She is currently a freshman at Yale University.

Executive Director

Anabella Laudicina

Executive Director · Texas A&M University

Growing up in a half-Vietnamese, half-Italian household, Anabella developed a deep appreciation for tradition — and for what's lost when cultures are left out of the conversation. She joined The Imagination Project in 2026, bringing experience across the arts and medicine.

She founded a local Leo Club chapter that scaled across Austin, and is now a neuroscience major at Texas A&M University.
